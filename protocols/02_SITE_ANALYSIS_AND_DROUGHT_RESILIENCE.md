# Protocol: Arboricultural Site Analysis and Heat/Drought Resilience Assessment

**Author:** George Steward, ISA Certified Arborist (`TX-362025A`) | MNR Candidate, Oregon State University  
**Standards Reference:** `ANSI A300` (Part 1, Part 2 Soil Management, Part 5 Construction Management), `ASTM D3441`

---

## 1. Objective

Before prescribing tree planting, canopy pruning, or soil remediation across urban, peri-urban, or wildland-urban interface (`WUI`) parcels, a practitioner must diagnose the site's physical, hydrological, and climatic constraints. Cosmetic symptoms in the upper crown almost always originate in the subterranean root zone (`rhizosphere`).

---

## 2. Four-Tier Diagnostic Site Evaluation Matrix

### Tier 1: Macro- and Micro-Climatic Stressors
* **Vapor Pressure Deficit (`VPD`) & Thermal Load:** Evaluate parcel exposure to reflected radiant heat (asphalt, south/west building facades, limestone hardpan) and prevailing desiccating winds.
* **Precipitation Regime vs. Evapotranspiration:** Compare seasonal rainfall distribution against peak summer potential evapotranspiration (`PET`). Across Arizona and Central Texas field trials, sustained heat waves (`> 100°F` for `30+ days`) trigger xylem cavitation when soil matric potential drops below species-specific wilting thresholds.

### Tier 2: Edaphic and Subterranean Physics
* **Penetrometer Resistance Profile (`ASTM D3441`):** Measure soil mechanical resistance in `3-inch` increments to `18 inches` depth across the Critical Root Zone (`CRZ`, `1 ft radius per 1 in DBH`) and outer drip line.
  * `< 150 PSI`: Unimpeded feeder root elongation and macropore aeration.
  * `150 to 300 PSI`: Restricted root growth; reduced water infiltration.
  * `> 300 PSI`: Root elongation arrested; severe hypoxia risk.
* **Parent Material & Soil Series:** Identify whether the substrate is shallow fractured karst limestone (Edwards Plateau), high-shrink-swell smectitic clay vertisol (Blackland Prairie), or alluvial terrace loam.
* **Soil Chemistry & Organic Matter:** Verify soil pH (calcareous `pH 7.8 to 8.4` frequently induces iron/manganese chlorosis in non-adapted species), electrical conductivity (`EC`), and organic matter percentage via laboratory assay before applying any mineral amendment.

### Tier 3: Root Collar and Structural Anchorage (`ANSI A300 Part 2 & Part 9`)
* **Root Flare Visibility:** Inspect whether primary structural buttress roots flare visibly at grade. A trunk entering the ground like a utility pole indicates grade fill burial (`4 to 14+ inches`) or nursery deep-planting.
* **Stem-Girdling Roots (`SGRs`) & Decay Indicators:** Probe the root collar (or perform non-destructive supersonic air excavation at `90 PSI`) to check for encircling roots compressing vascular cambium, basal cavities, or fungal fruiting bodies (*Ganoderma*, *Armillaria*, *Inonotus*).

### Tier 4: Stand Composition, Pathogens, and Statutory Context
* **Pathogen Vector Window (`Ceratocystis fagacearum` / Oak Wilt):** In Central Texas oak savannas, verify proximity to active Oak Wilt mortality centers. Prohibit non-emergency pruning of *Quercus* species during peak nitidulid beetle vector months (`February 1 through June 30`) and seal all wounds immediately upon cutting.
* **Municipal Tree Protection & Mitigation Compliance:** Cross-reference all stems against municipal DBH protection tiers (Protected, Significant, Legacy, Heritage) and Critical Root Zone impervious cover / disturbance limits before finalizing site development or treatment plans.
